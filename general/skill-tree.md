# Books
| Root | Foundation book(s) | Why I recommend it |
|---|---|---|
| **1. Formal & Computational Systems** | **The Princeton Companion to Mathematics**, ed. Timothy Gowers; then **Computer Science: An Overview, 13th ed.**, Brookshear & Brylow | The first gives you a map of modern mathematics; the second is deliberately breadth-first across computer science. Pearson's current Global 13th edition is a 2026 publication.  |
| **2. Natural & Life Sciences** | **The Sciences: An Integrated Approach, 9th ed.** — James Trefil & Robert Hazen | Probably the closest match to your knowledge-tree philosophy: it deliberately integrates **physics, chemistry, astronomy, Earth science, and biology** instead of teaching them as isolated subjects. ([Google Books][1]) |
| **3. Human & Health Sciences** | **Guyton and Hall Textbook of Medical Physiology, 15th ed.** — John Hall & Michael Hall | A serious foundation for understanding how the human body functions before branching into pathology, medicine, neuroscience, pharmacology, etc. The 15th edition is the current edition from Elsevier. ([Elsevier Shop][2]) |
| **4. Social & Behavioral Sciences** | **Social Science: An Introduction to the Study of Society, 18th ed.** — David Colander & Elgin Hunt | Excellent fit for your tree because it intentionally connects anthropology, psychology, sociology, demography, politics, economics, international relations, institutions and culture. ([Routledge][3]) |
| **5. Humanities & Culture** | **The Humanities: Culture, Continuity, and Change, 4th ed.** — Henry Sayre | Gives you historical context and connections across civilization, culture, ideas and the humanities rather than treating them as disconnected facts. ([Pearson][4]) |
| **6. Arts & Creative Practice** | **The Story of Art** — E. H. Gombrich | One of the strongest introductory maps of visual-art development from early art through the modern era. It is much better as an orientation book than immediately memorizing artists and movements. ([Phaidon][5]) |
| **7. Engineering & Technology** | **Engineering Fundamentals: An Introduction to Engineering, 7th ed.** — Saeed Moaveni; optionally **The Way Things Work Now** — David Macaulay | Moaveni gives you the engineering mindset, problem solving, physical principles and major engineering specializations. Macaulay gives you an unusually intuitive visual understanding of machines and technologies. ([Cengage Asia][6]) |
| **8. Applied Professions & Practical Life** | **The Knowledge: How to Rebuild Our World from Scratch** — Lewis Dartnell; then **The Personal MBA, 10th Anniversary ed.** — Josh Kaufman | Dartnell connects agriculture, materials, energy, manufacturing, food, basic medicine and technologies into functioning civilization. Kaufman gives you an accessible map of business and organizational practice. ([Penguin Random House][7]) |
| **Meta-Knowledge** | **The Craft of Research, 5th ed.** — Booth et al.; **How to Read a Book** — Adler & Van Doren | These teach you how to investigate questions, evaluate evidence, construct arguments, read analytically and learn from difficult books—the machinery you need for studying all the other roots. The current *Craft of Research* edition was published in 2024. ([University of Chicago Press][8]) |
